# Editorial intelligence in practice

Use privately while drafting; return prose rather than worksheets. These are editorial methods, not empirical proof or a requirement to expose internal reasoning.

## Knowledge-to-writing translation
Select a mechanism only when it answers a question raised by the material. Record the question, relevant observation, mechanism, missing link, and prose destination. Translate a concept into an actor doing something under a constraint. For example, instead of naming operating leverage, calculate incremental operating profit divided by incremental revenue when comparable; explain which costs actually changed if the source supplies them. Do not infer fixed versus variable cost from two aggregate observations alone.

## Mechanism and identification
Separate accounting identities, measured associations, causal evidence and forecasts. For a causal claim identify the initial change, affected actor, constraint or incentive, intermediate behavior, outcome and lag. Check whether each link is observed, inferred or unknown. Compare the strongest competing explanation and name the observation that distinguishes it. Do not force a counterargument into an article when it adds no information. Avoid treating an absent measurement as zero.

## Authorial judgment
Capture the author's thesis, distinctive inference, evidence and intended implication before editing. Preserve supported judgments even if controversial. When evidence contradicts a premise, correct it and reconstruct only the defensible part of the argument; do not silently attribute a new opinion to the author. Keep forecasts conditional where the causal link is not established. A decisive conclusion can identify the one variable that matters without predicting its value.

## Compression and paragraph gain
Inventory material propositions with entity, period, metric, unit, denominator, polarity, modality and attribution. Map each to a destination sentence; merge only equivalent propositions. Reverse-check every new sentence against the source or a reproducible calculation. Keep independent evidence even when it supports the same conclusion. For each paragraph identify what the reader learns that was unavailable before it, and why the next paragraph needs it. Remove repeated conclusions, not necessary bridges or narrative rhythm. Compare length using the same counting convention; a shorter text that loses evidence fails.

## Adaptive prose
Financial: move from numbers to economic consequences and valuation conditions; do not infer a valuation without price and basis. Macro: distinguish stocks, flows, expectations and realized policy. Political: distinguish authority, enforcement, incentives and documented outcomes. Technical: explain inputs, actual execution, outputs and failure cases. Fiction: use viewpoint, choice, consequence and scene rhythm; factual claim ledgers do not replace character continuity. Chinese: prefer explicit subjects and concrete verbs over repeated abstract slogans. English: choose precise verbs and natural sentence lengths; do not replace banned words with equally empty synonyms.

## Multi-source synthesis and second-order reasoning
Build a private source-to-claim matrix: origin, reporting period, scope, unit, independence, observation and limitation. Repeated reporting of one disclosure is one origin, not corroboration. Reconcile definitions before combining figures; if reconciliation fails, retain separate series. Organize by analytical question, not by source. Use each source for the claim it can actually establish.

Trace the first effect into a specific actor's next decision, the constraint on that decision, the resulting effect on another actor and any feedback into the original variable. Stop where the evidence stops; a possible response is not an observed response. For example, a higher funding rate raises refinancing cost only for debt that reprices or matures; reduced investment is a subsequent behavioral possibility, not an accounting identity. Check timing, contractual terms, offsets and capacity before asserting it.

## Originality and competing explanations
Derive a new comparison, reconciliation, marginal calculation or implication from the source packet. State its basis and distinguish it from reported facts. Do not call a paraphrase original analysis. Test the strongest evidence-supported alternative at the point where it challenges the thesis: what it explains, what it fails to explain, and which observation would discriminate. If the alternative wins, revise the thesis. Do not manufacture an opposing view or force symmetrical treatment.

## Cross-paragraph reasoning and density
Give each paragraph an input inherited from the preceding argument, a distinct contribution and an implication used later. A transition may be necessary without adding a fact. Use a deletion test: if removing a paragraph changes neither evidence, reasoning, qualification nor reader comprehension, merge or remove it. Compare lengths under one convention and preserve all material propositions, including contrary evidence. Never compress by dropping the denominator, period, attribution or causal boundary.

## Chinese and English editorial practice
Chinese: lead with the specific actor and action; use natural Chinese clause order rather than translated English nominalizations. Prefer “经营利润减少2亿元” to “盈利能力呈现承压态势”. Avoid repeated “不是……而是……” contrasts, but retain a contrast that changes the meaning. Use percentage points for margin changes and preserve units such as 万、亿 and currency. English: prefer specific subjects and active verbs where agency is known; use passive voice when agency is unknown or irrelevant. Distinguish percent from percentage points, reported from adjusted earnings, and cash flow from profit. Avoid automatic synonym substitution and forced sentence-length variation. Both languages: preserve quoted wording, technical terms and author voice; do not impose financial argument structure on fiction.

## First principles and materiality
Start with the decision the reader needs to make, the outcome to explain, and its unit and horizon. Decompose it into an identity or physical constraint where available, then identify which terms can actually change and who controls them. For a business, distinguish revenue = volume × realized price (for a homogeneous product) from the behavioral claim that raising price increases revenue. For a heterogeneous business use a sum across products; do not hide mix changes. For policy, separate legal authority from resources and compliance. For technology, distinguish a theoretical bound from measured performance.

Rank a variable as decisive when a plausible change in it could reverse the thesis, alter the decision, or materially change timing. Use measured sensitivity where available; otherwise state the qualitative dependency, not an invented threshold. Keep independent counterevidence even when it is inconvenient. Do not equate materiality with the largest percentage change: a small recurring cost can dominate a large one-off item. Background belongs only where it makes a decisive variable intelligible.

## Causal identification and dynamic systems
For each causal assertion ask what would have happened without the alleged cause. Check timing, selection, common causes, reverse causality, measurement changes and plausible alternatives. A before/after comparison alone does not identify an effect. If a credible comparison group or experiment exists, examine its assumptions and scope; do not claim identification from the method name. Use association language when the design does not support causality.

Track a stock using opening stock + inflows − outflows + other adjustments = closing stock. Align periods and units; distinguish capacity from utilization, debt balance from debt service, and cash balance from cash generation. Separate contractual lags from behavioral lags. For feedback, specify direction, actor, delay and limiting constraint. A reinforcing loop is not evidence of unlimited growth. Never convert an illustrative scenario into a reported outcome.

## Contradictory evidence and framework conflicts
When two figures disagree, first compare entity, scope, denominator, period, currency, adjustment policy, publication vintage and original source. Reconcile only with a documented bridge; otherwise show the relevant difference and how it affects the conclusion. Prefer primary records for their own measurements, but do not automatically privilege a management explanation over independent evidence. A later revision may supersede an earlier estimate only for the same series and scope. Do not average incompatible measures or count repeated reports as independent corroboration.

When frameworks conflict, identify their assumptions, target outcome, time horizon and domain. A liquidity framework and a solvency framework may answer different questions; a short-run pricing response and long-run substitution may coexist. Where they make incompatible predictions under the same conditions, test the discriminating observation. Follow the primary skill for structure, secondary skills for compatible expression, and factual integrity above both. Do not force a synthesis when the evidence favors one explanation.

## Intellectual compression and authorial voice
Preserve the minimum complete argument: decisive evidence, inference bridge, conclusion, and material qualification. Remove restatements and generic setup before removing evidence. Combine calculations with their implication where readable. Keep the strongest contrary evidence and the author's original analytical move. Compare before and after with the same length convention and a proposition ledger; never claim compression merely because fewer paragraphs remain.

Identify voice through stance, cadence, specificity, preferred framing and characteristic distinctions, not through a list of adjectives. Preserve the author's supported judgment and intentional rhythm; correct false premises and disclose consequential changes. Do not mechanically replace every contrast, passive construction or technical term. In fiction, preserve viewpoint and character-specific diction; materiality means narrative consequence, not financial significance.

## Research and strategic insight
Use this route for research, investment theses and strategic commentary, not routine copyediting. First state the outcome, decision, horizon and comparison baseline. Find a discrepancy between observed outcomes and a documented expectation, or between two compatible measures. Rank candidate questions by decision relevance, evidence availability and ability to distinguish competing explanations. Do not claim a market consensus or mispricing without evidence of expectations or prices.

### Discover variables and binding constraints
Build a short private worksheet: observed result | candidate driver | observable proxy | competing driver | discriminating evidence | implication. Derive residuals only from compatible identities; a residual is not proof of its cause. For example, revenue growth without profit growth identifies an increase in total operating expenses, not which expense caused it. Distinguish measured drivers from latent variables and identify a feasible observation that would separate them. Stop when additional detail cannot change the judgment.

Identify the actor, objective, available resources, contractual rights, feasible alternatives, adjustment cost and time horizon. A constraint is binding only when relaxing it would improve the relevant outcome, given other constraints. Test whether another bottleneck would replace it. Capacity is not output, announced capital is not deployed capital, and legal authority is not compliance. Avoid attributing intent from outcomes alone.

### Marginal economics
Compute incremental revenue, incremental operating cost and incremental operating profit on aligned periods and scope. Incremental operating margin = change in operating profit / change in revenue, only when the denominator is meaningful; negative or near-zero revenue changes need explicit interpretation. Do not call this a causal return on new investment. Incremental ROIC requires attributable after-tax operating profit and comparable incremental invested capital; if absent, do not substitute capex or total revenue. Separate sunk cost, avoidable cost, opportunity cost, working capital, maintenance and growth capex when supported. Compare marginal return with the appropriate cost of capital only on compatible risk, tax and time bases. For policy or technical systems, use the equivalent marginal resource cost and incremental outcome.

### Strategic interaction and forward judgment
Map actors separately: objective, information, feasible actions, constraints, switching cost and observable response. Compare the strongest feasible alternative to the proposed action. Trace how one actor's choice changes another actor's payoff or options; distinguish an announced action from an executed response. Do not assume equilibrium, retaliation, collusion or intent without support. For feedback, identify the limiting resource and delay. A prediction should name the trigger, mechanism, horizon, expected observable direction and boundary condition. If the evidence supports only a conditional conclusion, state the condition once at the relevant link; do not invent probabilities, dates or numerical thresholds. Distinguish a forecast from a sensitivity calculation and from a reported fact.

### Falsification and editorial compression
Before drafting, state the thesis and strongest competing explanation in testable terms. Identify an observation that would reverse the thesis, an observation that would weaken it, and an observation that would merely update timing. Specify the metric, scope, source and horizon where available; if no justified threshold exists, use a directional comparison. Do not use a tautological test or an unavailable observation as if it were evidence. Revisit the thesis when contrary evidence is material; do not move the goalposts after seeing results. In the article integrate the most important test into the argument rather than appending a generic risk list.

Compress by organizing paragraphs around decisions: decisive observation, discriminating mechanism, strategic consequence and test. Merge shared premises; preserve independent evidence, counterarguments and boundary conditions. A shorter article fails if it drops a material fact or causal bridge. Measure length within the same language and counting convention. Keep the author's supported thesis and voice; deliver the article, not the worksheet. For fiction use character objectives and feasible choices only when useful; do not impose investment metrics on narrative.

### Worked boundary example (synthetic)
Revenue rises from 100 to 120, operating profit falls from 20 to 18, all in millions on the same scope. Operating expenses rise from 80 to 102; incremental operating margin is −2/20 = −10%. This identifies deteriorating aggregate incremental economics, not the causal profitability of a particular product. If management attributes costs to launch spending, request recurring cost and product-level contribution evidence. A rival's price cut is a possible response only if supported by its incentives and capacity; it is not an observed event. A subsequent comparable period with rising revenue and positive incremental operating profit would weaken the deterioration thesis; recurring cost evidence is needed to distinguish recovery from a mix shift. No valuation claim follows without price and expectations data.

## Acceptance
Check every material fact, calculation, causal bridge, authorial judgment and paragraph contribution against the draft. If the brief requires compression, verify the length and material retention separately. Do not claim that a lexical scan or a self-assigned score establishes editorial quality.
