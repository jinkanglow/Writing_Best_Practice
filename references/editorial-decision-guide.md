# Editorial decision guide

Use this guide to turn research into a readable argument, not to impose a fixed chapter template. Examples below are invented teaching cases, not reported facts.

## Financial research
Start with the investment question and the market expectation being challenged. Trace volume × realized price to revenue, then incremental cost, operating profit, reinvestment, working capital and cash flow. Distinguish reported, adjusted and estimated metrics; separate stock from flow and cash expense from noncash expense. Explain who captures economic value and why the competitive structure permits it. A valuation conclusion needs a price, earnings or cash-flow basis and a time horizon; growth alone does not establish undervaluation.

Example: revenue rises from 100 to 120 while operating profit falls from 20 to 18. Margin falls from 20% to 15%, a five-percentage-point decline. The numbers establish weaker operating conversion, not its cause. Examine cost mix and incremental investment before assigning blame. If capital expenditure also rises, inspect cash flow separately rather than treating profit as cash.

## Macroeconomic commentary
Identify the shock, transmission channel, affected balance sheets, adjustment lag and observable outcome. Separate levels, changes and rates of change; nominal from real; liquidity stocks from flows. For market moves distinguish policy expectations, realized policy, positioning and risk premia. Do not infer a causal relationship from timing alone. Specify which subsequent observation would discriminate between competing explanations.

## Political commentary
Distinguish legal authority, actual implementation, incentives and capacity. Map who decides, who executes, who can obstruct, and who bears the cost. Separate documented action from inferred motive. Test whether an apparent concession changes enforceable obligations or merely rhetoric. Avoid assigning private intentions without evidence.

## Technical writing
Start with the actual user problem, inputs, constraints and expected output. Explain the mechanism at the level needed to reproduce or evaluate it. Distinguish implemented behavior from design intent, benchmark conditions from production performance, and failure handling from the happy path. Give a worked example and a meaningful boundary case where useful. Never claim a test was run unless it was.

## Paragraph decision
Before keeping a paragraph, identify its new contribution. If it repeats the previous paragraph's conclusion, merge or remove it. If it introduces a new causal claim, identify supporting evidence and the intermediate mechanism. If it makes a forecast, state the condition and relevant observable signal. Preserve a necessary recap when it enables a new inference.

## Revision example
Weak: “The company is undergoing a transformative shift. This shift is significant for its future. It is important to note that revenue growth does not necessarily mean profitability.”
Better (using the invented figures above): “Revenue rose 20%, but operating profit fell 10%. Operating margin dropped five percentage points to 15%. The investment case now depends on whether the additional costs are temporary or recur with each new sale.”
The revision retains the distinction between growth and profitability, adds a reproducible calculation and identifies the next research question without inventing a cause.

## Acceptance
A reader should be able to identify the thesis, evidence, mechanism, competing explanation where material, and practical implication. A claim unsupported by the available sources must be removed, qualified or researched. No numerical score substitutes for checking these properties in the actual article.
