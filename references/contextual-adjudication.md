# Contextual adjudication

Parse directives with their original wording, condition and source offset. Unparsed lines require interpretation; never silently discard them. A conditional prohibition is not a universal ban. For example, “If quoted, must not edit” protects quotations rather than preventing all editing.

Resolve competing requirements by preserving factual integrity first, then the primary framework, then contextual expression. Record the resolution and a quote from the final draft. A quoted phrase alone does not establish semantic compliance; review the surrounding passage.

For each fictional scene, enumerate incoming dependencies and identify the action that changes a later decision. An opening scene may have no predecessor. An inventory is not proof that all causal relationships have been discovered.

For nonfiction, compare each claim with the exact source passage. Check subject, metric, period, units, conditions, polarity and causal strength. A delivery estimate cannot by itself establish revenue. A source quote that supports only part of a claim requires revision rather than approval. Preserve material caveats once, at the point where they affect the inference.

Reviewer trust is managed outside the submitted review. Reject expired, revoked or duplicate keys. Establish identity and independence separately; signatures only authenticate possession of a trusted key. Synthetic cases must not be counted as independent real-world reviews.
