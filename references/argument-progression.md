# Argument progression and evidence boundaries

Before drafting, assign each section a distinct question and a new contribution: evidence, mechanism, comparison, calculation or implication. Keep the strongest version of a recurring claim. A later recurrence must change its scope, introduce new evidence or draw a new consequence. A summary can legitimately recall the thesis.

Place a material qualification next to the inference it limits. Do not repeat generic disclaimers at every transition. A delivery estimate measures availability, not recognized revenue; establish this once, then analyze the revenue bridge with the appropriate evidence. Do not turn uncertainty into certainty by deleting a necessary qualification.

Example (illustrative): “Delivery times fell from 72 to 37 days. This does not necessarily mean demand weakened. The distinction matters.” becomes “Delivery times fell by 35 days. Distinguishing higher supply from weaker demand requires shipment and order data.” A subsequent section must add those data or explain a separate mechanism, not repeat the caveat.

Review length against the original, preserve material facts and author contributions, and remove empty transitions before cutting evidence. No fixed length or vocabulary quota overrides accuracy. Return the fused article, not the review ledger, unless requested.
