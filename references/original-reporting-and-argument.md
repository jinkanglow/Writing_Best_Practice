# Original reporting and argument ownership

Before drafting, identify the author's question, independently supported thesis, source facts, original calculations and their inputs, and what would overturn the thesis. Do not invent reporting, interviews or author experience. A source summary is not independent reporting. Attribute source-derived findings even when paraphrased. If only a source summary exists, label the work as analysis of that source, or request further evidence. Never imply that AI assistance is permitted by a publisher without checking its current rules; disclose assistance where required and do not submit where prohibited.

## Argument progression

Assign each paragraph a claim ID and a distinct contribution: evidence, mechanism, calculation, counterexample, or implication. Merge paragraphs that only restate a prior conclusion. A recap may serve a different audience need; preserve necessary context. Use a qualification once at the point where it changes the inference. Preserve independent limitations even when their grammatical form repeats. Replace transition scaffolding with the actual causal connection, not synonyms. Do not add arbitrary stylistic variation to game a detector.

## Source independence review

Compare draft and source outlines, claim order, distinctive phrasing, examples and conclusions. Document which elements come from each source and which are independently developed. Shared facts alone do not establish copying. A new calculation must show inputs, units, formula and limitations; a new interpretation must explain the reasoning. Do not invent additional sources to dilute overlap. If the source is unavailable, mark comparison incomplete. Human editorial review is required for conceptual dependence and publication rights.

## Delivery

Apply the primary writing framework and language constraints together. Return the complete fused article by default, not a process report. Keep audit records separate unless requested. Do not omit material uncertainty or required disclosures.
